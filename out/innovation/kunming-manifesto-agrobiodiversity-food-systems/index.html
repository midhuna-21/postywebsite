Kunming Manifesto Calls for Agrobiodiversity to Transform Food Systems

  • by Lauren Jacobs
  • October 5, 2025

The Kunming Manifesto recognizes the need for agrobiodiversity to help build resilient, sustainable and fair food systems globally.

The Kunming Manifesto emphasizes the critical role of agrobiodiversity in shaping the future of global food systems, asserting that the diversity of crops and animal breeds is the foundation for both environmental sustainability and long-term food security. By promoting agrobiodiversity, the manifesto aims to reduce dependence on monoculture farming practices, which are often linked to soil degradation, biodiversity loss, and heightened vulnerability to pests, diseases, and climate-related shocks. Instead, it advocates for diversified agricultural systems that can safeguard ecosystems, enhance resilience, and provide a more stable foundation for food production in the face of accelerating climate change. The manifesto underscores that monoculture may deliver short-term efficiency but undermines sustainability, while agrobiodiversity offers adaptive capacity, ecological balance, and stronger food security. Central to the Kunming Manifesto is the idea that maintaining and increasing genetic diversity—both in plant species and livestock breeds—is essential for sustainable agricultural practices worldwide. In many countries, however, agricultural systems are still constrained by limited genetic resources and conservation strategies that do not adequately prioritize diversity. These limitations vary widely depending on a country’s policies, conservation programs, and capacity for sustainable agriculture, creating uneven progress toward global food system resilience. Advocates of the manifesto highlight that future agricultural transformation must include deliberate efforts to broaden the genetic base of farming, not only to improve yields but also to ensure adaptability to shifting environmental and socio-economic conditions. The Kunming Manifesto calls on governments, researchers, policymakers, and farming communities to collaborate in creating systemic changes that embrace diversity as a guiding principle.

Governments are urged to develop supportive policies and invest in research that conserves traditional crop varieties, protects indigenous livestock breeds, and integrates scientific innovation with local knowledge. Researchers are tasked with advancing studies that explore resilient crop varieties, adaptive breeding techniques, and ecosystem-friendly farming models. Farmers and communities are positioned as essential agents of change, as their traditional knowledge, on-the-ground practices, and stewardship of local biodiversity remain indispensable for sustaining diverse agricultural landscapes. The manifesto is seen by observers as not merely an environmental declaration but also as a call for social and economic transformation. It stresses that equitable food systems cannot be achieved without addressing inequalities in access to diverse seeds, breeds, and agricultural resources. Many rural and marginalized communities are disproportionately affected by monoculture-based food systems and the vulnerabilities they create. By embedding agrobiodiversity at the core of food systems, the manifesto envisions empowering these communities, enhancing livelihoods, and creating fairer opportunities within global agriculture. A key point of the Kunming Manifesto is its focus on climate resilience. As global climate conditions continue to change—with rising temperatures, altered rainfall patterns, and increased frequency of extreme weather events—the need for resilient food systems becomes more urgent.

Agrobiodiversity provides the raw materials for adaptation, allowing crops and livestock to adjust to evolving conditions. This diversity acts as a natural insurance policy against uncertainty, enabling farmers to continue producing food despite environmental stressors. In this sense, the manifesto aligns climate adaptation with food security, linking ecological stewardship directly with human survival. The proposal also emphasizes conservation as both a scientific and cultural endeavor. Traditional seed banks, indigenous farming methods, and community-driven conservation projects are highlighted as invaluable resources for sustaining agrobiodiversity. By strengthening conservation initiatives and integrating them into modern farming, the Kunming Manifesto suggests that societies can safeguard valuable genetic resources for future generations. This conservation not only preserves ecological integrity but also protects cultural heritage tied to diverse agricultural practices. Another dimension of the manifesto is its global outlook. It frames agrobiodiversity as a shared resource and collective responsibility, requiring cooperation among nations and institutions.

As food demand grows and climate risks intensify, no single country can address food system challenges in isolation. International collaboration, fair trade policies, and shared research platforms are essential for creating resilient and equitable global food systems. The Kunming Manifesto thus acts as a rallying point for global solidarity in agriculture, linking local action with international frameworks for sustainability and climate response. In conclusion, the Kunming Manifesto proposes a transformative vision for the future of agriculture, centered on agrobiodiversity as the key to climate resilience, environmental sustainability, and food equity. It calls for systemic change, urging governments, scientists, farmers, and communities to work together in preserving and expanding the genetic diversity of crops and livestock. By reducing reliance on monocultures and strengthening conservation efforts, the manifesto envisions food systems that are more resilient to climate change, more inclusive for marginalized populations, and more sustainable for generations to come. Observers interpret this proposal as a vital step toward preparing the world’s food systems for future challenges, ensuring that advances in agriculture support both ecological balance and human well-being on a global scale.
