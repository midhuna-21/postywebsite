13:T7a0,The Kunming Manifesto emphasizes the critical role of agrobiodiversity in shaping the future of global food systems, asserting that the diversity of crops and animal breeds is the foundation for both environmental sustainability and long-term food security.  By promoting agrobiodiversity, the manifesto aims to reduce dependence on monoculture farming practices, which are often linked to soil degradation, biodiversity loss, and heightened vulnerability to pests, diseases, and climate-related shocks.  Instead, it advocates for diversified agricultural systems that can safeguard ecosystems, enhance resilience, and provide a more stable foundation for food production in the face of accelerating climate change.  The manifesto underscores that monoculture may deliver short-term efficiency but undermines sustainability, while agrobiodiversity offers adaptive capacity, ecological balance, and stronger food security. 

Central to the Kunming Manifesto is the idea that maintaining and increasing genetic diversity—both in plant species and livestock breeds—is essential for sustainable agricultural practices worldwide.  In many countries, however, agricultural systems are still constrained by limited genetic resources and conservation strategies that do not adequately prioritize diversity.  These limitations vary widely depending on a country’s policies, conservation programs, and capacity for sustainable agriculture, creating uneven progress toward global food system resilience.  Advocates of the manifesto highlight that future agricultural transformation must include deliberate efforts to broaden the genetic base of farming, not only to improve yields but also to ensure adaptability to shifting environmental and socio-economic conditions.
