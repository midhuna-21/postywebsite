2025 Performance Report by WNA: Evaluating the nuclear energy sector

  • by Lauren Jacobs
  • 4th September 2025

The World Nuclear Association's 2025 report is an analysis on global nuclear energy performance, trends, and prospects.

The World Nuclear Association (WNA) has released its 2025 Performance Report, providing one of the most detailed, forward-looking assessments of the nuclear power sector on a global scale. This annual report offers comprehensive coverage of the ways in which nuclear energy continues to evolve, situating the industry not only in terms of technological advances and investment patterns but also in the broader global context of clean energy transition and climate policy development. It serves as both a snapshot of the current state of nuclear power and a roadmap for the future, identifying areas of opportunity and addressing the sector’s challenges as it aims to play a more central role in global decarbonization efforts. The report underscores the immense potential of nuclear power as a low-carbon, reliable energy source, while at the same time acknowledging the complexities of plant aging, regulatory frameworks, public perception, and financial investment that continue to shape the sector’s trajectory. One of the central themes of the 2025 report is the growing recognition of nuclear energy’s importance in climate strategies. With rising global demand for electricity and increasing pressure to meet carbon neutrality targets, nuclear power has emerged as a viable and essential contributor to decarbonization. Unlike intermittent renewable sources such as wind and solar, nuclear offers continuous baseload power with minimal greenhouse gas emissions. The report highlights data comparing electricity generation trends across regions, noting that countries with strong nuclear programs—such as France, South Korea, and Canada—are maintaining relatively low carbon intensity in their energy systems. By contrast, countries without significant nuclear capacity are often more reliant on fossil fuels, making their transition to clean energy slower and more complex. In this sense, the WNA frames nuclear power as not simply one option among many but as a necessary pillar of future low-carbon energy systems. The report devotes considerable attention to the regulatory and safety dimensions of nuclear power, both of which are crucial to its acceptance and expansion. Over the past several decades, nuclear safety has improved significantly, with stringent international standards and oversight ensuring that new plants are designed and operated with maximum precaution. The WNA notes that the industry’s safety record in recent years has been strong, with fewer incidents and increasing emphasis on transparency. However, safety remains one of the most scrutinized aspects of nuclear power, and the report acknowledges that maintaining public trust will require ongoing vigilance and investment in safety systems. Importantly, the report points out that safety is not merely a regulatory requirement but a driver of technological innovation, leading to advanced reactor designs that incorporate passive safety features, smaller modular systems, and enhanced emergency preparedness. Technological advancement is another key focus of the 2025 Performance Report. The nuclear sector is experiencing a wave of innovation, from small modular reactors (SMRs) to advanced Generation IV systems, which promise greater efficiency, enhanced safety, and reduced waste production. SMRs, in particular, are highlighted as a transformative technology, offering flexible deployment options, shorter construction times, and lower upfront costs compared to traditional large-scale reactors. These innovations could expand nuclear energy’s reach into markets and regions that have previously lacked the capacity or resources to develop nuclear infrastructure. Furthermore, technological advances in waste management, decommissioning processes, and fuel recycling are helping to address long-standing challenges associated with nuclear power, offering new pathways toward sustainability and public acceptance. Financial and investment patterns also feature prominently in the WNA’s 2025 report. The nuclear industry requires significant upfront capital, and this has often been cited as a barrier to new projects. However, the report identifies several trends that are beginning to shift investment dynamics in favor of nuclear. Rising carbon prices, growing recognition of nuclear’s role in decarbonization, and supportive government policies in regions such as Asia and Europe are creating more favorable conditions for financing nuclear projects. Additionally, innovations like modular construction and standardized reactor designs are reducing costs and making nuclear more competitive with other low-carbon technologies. Still, the report emphasizes that access to financing remains uneven, with some countries benefiting from strong political support while others struggle to secure the necessary investment due to policy uncertainty or public opposition. Public perception and reputation management are also explored in depth. The report candidly acknowledges that despite nuclear power’s proven safety record and environmental benefits, public concerns remain a significant obstacle. Memories of past accidents, concerns about radioactive waste, and fears of proliferation continue to influence public opinion and, by extension, political decision-making. The WNA argues that overcoming these challenges requires not only continued emphasis on safety and transparency but also proactive communication strategies that clearly articulate nuclear’s role in addressing climate change, reducing emissions, and ensuring energy security. In this context, education and outreach are identified as essential tools for building broader public support and ensuring the long-term viability of the sector. Another area the report addresses is the aging of nuclear plants. Many reactors currently in operation were built decades ago, and while they have been consistently upgraded and maintained, the question of lifespan and replacement remains pressing. Extending the life of existing plants can provide immediate low-carbon energy at relatively low cost, but it requires careful planning, robust regulatory oversight, and investment in modernization. The WNA highlights several successful examples of life extension programs that have allowed plants to continue operating safely beyond their originally planned retirement dates. At the same time, it emphasizes the importance of balancing these extensions with investments in new capacity to ensure that the industry does not face a significant drop-off in production in the coming decades. The 2025 Performance Report also situates nuclear energy within the broader clean energy transition. While nuclear has unique advantages, it is most effective when integrated with other low-carbon technologies such as wind, solar, and hydro. The WNA emphasizes the importance of cooperation across the clean energy sector, pointing to hybrid systems that combine nuclear with renewables, as well as the role of nuclear in providing heat and hydrogen for industrial processes. This holistic approach positions nuclear not as a competitor to renewables but as a complementary technology that enhances overall system resilience, stability, and flexibility. Industry experts contributing to the report stress that the insights provided are not only relevant for the nuclear sector itself but also for policymakers, investors, and climate strategists. By analyzing global trends in electricity generation, investment, and policy, the WNA aims to provide valuable guidance for those tasked with shaping the energy systems of the future. For governments, the report highlights opportunities to leverage nuclear in meeting climate commitments and ensuring energy security. For investors, it provides evidence of nuclear’s long-term viability and potential for stable returns. For the broader energy community, it reinforces the message that achieving global climate goals will require a diverse mix of technologies, with nuclear playing a central role. The WNA anticipates that the 2025 Performance Report will directly influence strategic decisions within the nuclear industry and beyond. It calls for long-term planning, international cooperation, and proactive investment to ensure that nuclear energy continues to grow as a reliable and sustainable component of the global energy mix. By situating the nuclear sector in the wider context of decarbonization and clean energy strategies, the report provides a roadmap for policymakers and industry leaders who must make decisions today that will shape the sector for decades to come. In conclusion, the 2025 Performance Report from the World Nuclear Association presents a balanced and comprehensive picture of the nuclear sector. It highlights the potential of nuclear energy to be a cornerstone of global decarbonization while openly addressing the challenges of financing, public perception, plant aging, and regulatory complexity. The report makes clear that nuclear power has both the capability and the responsibility to contribute to climate solutions, but achieving this potential will require concerted effort across governments, industries, and communities. By offering data-driven insights, technological updates, and strategic guidance, the WNA’s report positions nuclear energy as not only relevant but indispensable in the quest for a sustainable, low-carbon future. The decisions made in the coming years, informed by reports such as this, will determine the scale and success of nuclear energy’s role in the global clean energy transition for decades ahead.
