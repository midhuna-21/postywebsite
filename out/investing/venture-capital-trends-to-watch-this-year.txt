12:T86d,Venture capital is always changing — funding priorities shift with market trends, technological evolution and changes in the global economy. One way for founders to understand venture capital trends is by observing the trends themselves, and only then transforming theirs businesses so that they can have the right conversations. One trend that is hard to ignore is the trend toward sustainable and climate-oriented investing. Startups in waste diversion, clean energy production, circular economy solutions and environmental technology are accessing investment capital from investors focused on feelings of well-being and, in many cases, addressing climate change in like to achieving market returns. Healthcare and biotech also remain robust verticals, especially with continuing innovation in digital health, telehealth and personalized medicine. Investors are investing in companies that can mitigate disruption, and rather than promising new ideas, they want companies that can produce or demonstrate positive outcomes. Geographical diversification is another emerging trend. Silicon Valley still reigns supreme, but venture capital is moving into new markets in Asia, Africa, and Latin America. These markets have less competition and a lot more white space, but founders must show how they can scale and can forecast how their product fits the local ecosystem. Another trend is the emphasis on committing money to early-stage and higher-risk fundings, primarily around AI, blockchain, and deep tech. VCs are motivated to back teams with strong visionary ideas, as long as the team can articulate those ideas into practicable next steps. Founders must stay on top of these trends. Finding a way to get your startup aligned with the sectors investors are putting money into increases your chance of engagement, but at the same time, you don't want to be swayed by trends and markets. Focus on building your business. One venture capitalist told me, Trends are important, but execution wins.
