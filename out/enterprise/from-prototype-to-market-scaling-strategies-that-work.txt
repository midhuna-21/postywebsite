12:T854,Either prototyping to product-market fit is one of the hardest stages for any startup. The lessons here can illustrate what to do - and what not to do - during scaling. Every founder must reach the create a product that customers buy phase from a positive signal of a prototype. The transition is rarely a linear one, however, there are several approaches to help make the transition less painful. First, validate demand. Many startups rush out to scale without validating that customers are going to pay. Early customer interviews, pilot programs and beta launch will provide important feedback that improves learning and saves both resources and time. Second, build systems before scaling. The ad hoc process of prototyping might just survive as a prototype, however, once you go public you need to have reliable processes - supply chain logistics, customer support, etc. Growth can show serious weakness without proper systems in place. Third, test leanly. Successful founders suggest primarily experimenting in a controlled environment. They suggest releasing features little by little and tracking adoption. They won't scale up until they see traction. Overbuild too soon is a common mistake that results in wasted resources. Fourth, align the team. Scaling is about people as much as it is about the product - people must have a common understanding of organizational priorities, and the team must have the breadth of culture to deal with the pressures of growth. Lastly, prepare for external challenges, which may include regulatory issues, competition, or capital needs. When startups are proactive about potential challenges, they put themselves in a great position to create differentiation. Case studies of other successful startups show that sustainable scaling is not about your metrics or speed. Fortunately, building strong foundations takes precedence over speed to reach them.
