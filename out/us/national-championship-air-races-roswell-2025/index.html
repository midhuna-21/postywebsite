National Championship Air Races Take Flight in Roswell, New Mexico

  • by Lauren Jacobs
  • 10th September 2025

The National Championship Air Races have relocated to Roswell, New Mexico, marking the first time in the event's 60-year history that it is held outside Reno, Nevada.

The National Championship Air Races, widely recognized as the nation’s premier head-to-head airplane racing event, is making a historic move to Roswell, New Mexico, marking the first time in its 60-year history that the event will take place outside of Reno, Nevada. This relocation is expected to draw tens of thousands of enthusiastic fans from across the country and internationally, eager to witness high-speed, low-altitude aerial competition and aerobatic displays. The decision to relocate from Reno stemmed from multiple safety concerns, including urban encroachment on the airport and limited available space for the event as the surrounding area developed over time. Over the five-day period of the races, pilots will fly at extremely low altitudes, sometimes as low as 50 feet above the ground, while reaching speeds approaching 500 miles per hour. The aircraft competing include self-built single-seat Formula 1 racers, which are propeller-driven airplanes specifically engineered for maximum speed and maneuverability, as well as aerobatic biplanes capable of performing complex aerial stunts and high-G turns that test both pilot skill and aircraft engineering. Roswell was selected over multiple candidate cities from six states, largely due to its expansive and unobstructed desert airspace, which provides ample room for high-speed racing with minimal risk to surrounding populations. The state government and local community have pledged significant support, including investments to develop the Roswell Air Center to accommodate the influx of spectators. Plans include grandstands capable of seating 9,500 patrons, along with necessary infrastructure improvements for parking, safety, and accessibility. Although this year will not feature World War II-era aircraft racing, attendees can still enjoy military flyovers, vintage aircraft displays, and aerobatic performances that showcase the skill of modern and historical pilots alike. The relocation comes in the wake of last year’s tragic Greenwich crash in Reno, where two highly experienced veteran pilots lost their lives, prompting organizers to re-evaluate safety protocols and consider alternative venues that could offer enhanced protection for both participants and spectators.

In Roswell, the expansive desert landscape allows for wider dispersal of spectators from the turns and corners of the racecourse, reducing risk exposure and enabling safer observation of the races from designated viewing areas. The course layout and airspace management have been designed to separate racing activity entirely from nearby residential areas, minimizing potential hazards and noise disruptions. Organizers have committed to collaborating with local civil authorities to ensure that all necessary permissions and safety measures are in place, adhering strictly to Federal Aviation Regulations (FAR) and FAA guidelines to maintain distances between racing aircraft, spectators, and infrastructure. The races will be conducted with FAA-accredited safety zones that guarantee appropriate separation from people, property, and sensitive areas, reflecting a robust approach to event management and risk mitigation. In addition to competitive racing, the event will include comprehensive aviation exhibits, pilot meet-and-greets, educational workshops, and interactive experiences designed to engage aviation enthusiasts of all ages. These elements aim to celebrate the history and culture of aviation while fostering interest in STEM (Science, Technology, Engineering, and Mathematics) education and careers in aerospace. Local businesses and tourism sectors are expected to experience a substantial economic boost from the event, as visitors require lodging, food, transportation, and entertainment, generating revenue and employment opportunities throughout the region. The state and city authorities have coordinated with the event organizers to facilitate smooth logistics, including traffic management, emergency response planning, and coordination with law enforcement and medical teams to ensure public safety. The move to Roswell also offers strategic advantages for international media coverage, as the city’s location allows for convenient access by air and road, as well as opportunities for live broadcasts and digital streaming to reach global audiences. The event’s planning committee has emphasized that safety remains the highest priority, implementing multiple layers of redundancy, including aerial surveillance, fire and rescue teams, and medical stations throughout the venue.

Pilots are required to undergo thorough pre-race inspections, flight briefings, and safety training to mitigate risks associated with high-speed, low-altitude racing. Aircraft are subject to rigorous technical checks and certifications, ensuring airworthiness and compliance with both racing standards and FAA regulations. Additionally, the organizers are implementing spectator education campaigns to inform attendees about safe viewing practices, prohibited areas, and emergency procedures to prevent accidents and facilitate orderly evacuation if needed. The relocation also allows the Air Races to expand its community engagement programs, including school visits, aviation camps, and public demonstrations of flight techniques, which aim to inspire the next generation of pilots and aerospace engineers. Organizers are actively working with local aviation clubs, veteran pilot associations, and aerospace companies to provide mentorship, internships, and networking opportunities for young enthusiasts. Media coverage of the event is expected to be extensive, with television networks, online streaming platforms, and social media channels providing live updates, interviews, and behind-the-scenes footage of the races and air shows. This heightened visibility is anticipated to enhance sponsorship opportunities for both the event and participating teams, attracting funding from aviation-related corporations, technology companies, and consumer brands seeking association with high-profile sporting events. The selection of Roswell also aligns with the city’s historical significance in aerospace and extraterrestrial culture, offering a unique backdrop for the races and potential synergy with tourism campaigns that highlight local heritage and attractions. Organizers are collaborating with hotels, restaurants, and cultural institutions to create comprehensive visitor packages that combine race attendance with exploration of the city’s landmarks and museums, maximizing the overall experience for attendees. Advanced safety technology, including radar tracking, GPS monitoring, and real-time telemetry, will be utilized to monitor aircraft positions, detect potential conflicts, and coordinate responses between air traffic controllers and race officials.

This technology ensures that all competitors operate within designated corridors and allows rapid intervention in case of emergency. The races will feature multiple categories and divisions, allowing pilots of varying experience levels to compete safely while maintaining the excitement and competitiveness of the event. From Formula 1 propeller racers to aerobatic planes, each category is designed to showcase unique aspects of aviation skill and aircraft performance. Environmental considerations have also been incorporated into the planning, with efforts to minimize noise impact, preserve desert ecosystems, and implement waste management and sustainability measures throughout the venue. Organizers have engaged environmental consultants to assess potential impacts and design mitigation strategies, such as designated pathways, controlled access to sensitive areas, and limitations on aircraft operations in specific zones. Local emergency services, including fire departments, medical responders, and law enforcement, have conducted joint drills and preparedness exercises in coordination with race organizers, ensuring readiness for any contingencies and seamless integration of public safety measures. Insurance coverage for the event has been carefully structured to account for the unique risks of air racing, including pilot liability, property damage, and spectator incidents, providing financial protection for both the organizers and the city. Community engagement initiatives include volunteer programs, local vendor participation, and public forums to solicit feedback and ensure that the event meets both safety and recreational expectations of residents. Additionally, the organizers are working with federal and state aviation authorities to secure necessary waivers, exemptions, and compliance certifications to operate the races safely within regulated airspace.
