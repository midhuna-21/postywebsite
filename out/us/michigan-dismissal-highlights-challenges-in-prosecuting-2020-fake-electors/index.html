Michigan Dismissal Shows Problems for Prosecutors of 2020 Fake Electors

  • by Lauren Jacobs
  • October 13, 2025

A judge in Michigan has dismissed charges against 15 people who were trying to falsify Donald Trump as the winner of the 2020 presidential election. The judge determined there was no fraudulent intent.

In a major and closely watched legal development, Ingham County District Court Judge Kristen D. Simmons has formally dismissed criminal charges against 15 individuals who had been accused of participating in a plan to falsely certify Donald Trump as the winner of Michigan’s 2020 presidential election. These defendants, several of whom are prominent figures within Michigan’s Republican Party, faced charges including forgery and conspiracy stemming from their submission of documents asserting that they were the state’s legitimate electors for Trump, despite the clear and decisive victory of Joe Biden in the state during the 2020 election. The charges arose after state prosecutors initiated an investigation into actions that were widely criticized as attempts to undermine the certified election results and subvert the democratic process. Judge Simmons’ ruling emphasized that the prosecution had failed to demonstrate that the defendants acted with fraudulent intent, noting instead that evidence suggested these individuals genuinely believed that irregularities had occurred during the election and that they were exercising what they viewed as their constitutional rights. In her ruling, Judge Simmons detailed the difficulty in establishing beyond a reasonable doubt that the defendants knowingly attempted to commit a criminal act, highlighting that personal belief in alleged election issues complicated the legal analysis and ultimately undermined the state’s case. The dismissal represents a significant setback for the state prosecutors who had invested considerable resources into the investigation and legal proceedings, and it is emblematic of a broader trend in battleground states such as Arizona, Georgia, and Nevada, where similar prosecutions have faced challenges in proving fraudulent intent, as defendants often claim to have acted in good faith based on their understanding of election procedures and perceived irregularities. Legal experts analyzing the ruling note that while the actions of the defendants were politically charged and generated widespread criticism, the judicial system must focus on intent and evidence rather than perceptions or public outrage.

Critics of the defendants argue that their actions represented a direct threat to the integrity of the democratic process, potentially eroding public confidence in elections and setting a troubling precedent for political interference. These critics contend that even if the defendants sincerely believed in irregularities, their attempts to submit alternative elector certifications circumvented the certified electoral process, undermining legal norms and democratic institutions. On the other hand, defenders of the dismissed defendants argue that the ruling underscores the constitutional right of citizens to engage in political processes, including raising concerns about election integrity. Supporters emphasize that the defendants were acting on their understanding of election laws and constitutional provisions, and that their intent was not malicious but motivated by their interpretation of legal rights and civic duty. Michigan Attorney General Dana Nessel publicly expressed disappointment regarding the dismissal and indicated that she is carefully considering the possibility of filing an appeal, noting that while legal precedent may make reversing the ruling difficult, she remains committed to exploring all available avenues to hold individuals accountable when state law is potentially violated. The AG’s statement also highlighted the ongoing tension between the enforcement of election laws and the challenge of proving intent in cases where defendants claim to act out of personal conviction rather than fraudulent purpose. The legal community has reacted with a mix of analysis and commentary, with scholars pointing out that this case illustrates the inherent difficulty in prosecuting politically motivated actions within the framework of criminal law. Experts emphasize that distinguishing between good-faith belief in election irregularities and intentional efforts to subvert certified results is often a complex legal task, requiring careful examination of communications, behavior, and context.

Beyond the immediate legal ramifications, the ruling has broader implications for election integrity and governance in Michigan and other battleground states. The outcome may influence how prosecutors approach similar cases in the future, particularly regarding evidentiary standards and the burden of proving intent. It also highlights the importance of clear legal definitions and guidance for actions related to elector certification and the submission of official election documents. The case has been closely followed by media outlets, political analysts, and the public, reflecting the national attention surrounding challenges to the 2020 presidential election results. Coverage has focused not only on the legal outcome but also on the political and social ramifications, including how such cases affect public confidence in electoral processes, trust in government institutions, and the broader discourse surrounding election integrity. Some observers have expressed concern that dismissals in cases like this one could embolden similar actions in future elections, potentially creating uncertainty about the enforceability of laws governing electoral certifications. Conversely, others view the ruling as an affirmation of the judicial principle that criminal liability must be clearly established through evidence of intent, rather than assumptions based on political disagreement or perceived wrongdoing. In addition to the legal and political considerations, the ruling has prompted reflection on the role of prosecutorial discretion, particularly in politically sensitive cases.

State prosecutors must balance the public interest in upholding election laws with the legal challenges inherent in proving fraudulent intent, as well as the potential reputational risks associated with high-profile prosecutions. Analysts note that this case may serve as a precedent for evaluating how similar allegations are handled in other jurisdictions, influencing both prosecutorial strategy and judicial interpretation of election-related statutes. The dismissal also reinforces the need for ongoing public education about the electoral process, the certification of results, and the legal mechanisms in place to address concerns about election integrity. Civic leaders, scholars, and advocacy organizations have emphasized that transparency, accurate information, and adherence to established legal procedures are essential to maintaining trust in democratic systems. As Michigan considers its next steps, including the potential for appeal, stakeholders will be closely monitoring developments, weighing the legal, political, and societal implications. The interplay between constitutional rights, state election laws, and criminal liability will remain a key point of discussion for policymakers, legal professionals, and the public alike. Overall, Judge Simmons’ decision to dismiss charges against the 15 individuals accused of falsely certifying Donald Trump as Michigan’s winner in the 2020 presidential election underscores the complexities of adjudicating politically sensitive cases, highlights the critical role of intent in criminal law, and reflects broader challenges faced by prosecutors in battleground states seeking to enforce election-related statutes while respecting constitutional protections. The ruling invites ongoing debate about accountability, democratic principles, and the balance between legal enforcement and civic participation, and it will likely continue to influence public discourse, legal strategy, and policy considerations surrounding elections in Michigan and across the United States.
